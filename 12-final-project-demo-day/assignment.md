# Session 12 — Demo Day Logistics & Judging

**Week 4, Session 12 of 12 — "Final Project Demo Day"**

There is no new lab assignment today. This note replaces the usual assignment sheet with the logistics and judging criteria for Demo Day.

## What today is

Today, teams run the robot they built and programmed across Sessions 10-11 through the **final challenge course**: a track combining line-following with an obstacle that must be detected and handled. This is the culmination of everything covered in the seminar — sensing, planning, and acting, all working together on real hardware, live in front of the class.

There is no new content to learn today. Come ready with your robot charged, your code loaded, and your team prepared to run.

## Running order / format

- The instructor posts a randomly-drawn running order at the start of class.
- Each team gets **approximately 5 minutes**:
  - ~1 minute to place the robot at the start line and get set up
  - ~2-3 minutes to run the course
  - ~1-2 minutes for brief Q&A from classmates and the instructor
- Each team gets **two attempts**; the better of the two runs counts toward completion credit.
- Teams not currently demoing should watch, take notes, and prepare at least one question to ask the team that's up.
- The instructor will call the next team up about 1 minute before their turn so they can get to the start line.

## Judging / completion criteria (recap of the Session 10 final project rubric)

Completion credit is pass/fail based on the same rubric issued with the Session 10 final project assignment — this is not a competitive ranking, and every team that meets the criteria succeeds:

1. **Line following** — Does the robot reliably detect and follow the taped line through the course?
2. **Obstacle detection & handling** — Does the robot detect the obstacle and execute an appropriate response (stopping, going around, or rerouting, depending on what the team implemented)?
3. **Course completion** — Does the robot complete the course reliably, reaching the end? (Partial credit applies for partial completion — the process and problem-solving matter, not just a flawless run.)

## Suggested wrap-up activity

After all teams have demoed, run a short group reflection (5-10 minutes) to close out the course:

- Go around (or use quick pair-shares) and have each team answer:
  - **What was the hardest bug you fixed this month, and how did you find it?**
  - **If you had one more week, what would you build or improve next?**
- Optional: have a few volunteers share out to the whole class.
- This is meant to be low-pressure — the goal is to have students articulate what they learned about debugging and the engineering process, not to grade the reflection itself.

## Instructor notes: collecting final deliverables

Before students leave, collect:

- **Final code submission** — each team's MicroPython project files (e.g., zipped folder or shared repo link) as run during their demo.
- **Short reflection writeup** — 3-5 sentences per student (not per team) answering the wrap-up prompts above (hardest bug fixed, what they'd build next). This can be submitted on paper, via a shared doc, or through the course LMS.
- **Demo notes** — instructor should record for each team: which attempt was used for credit, and a one-line note on whether each of the three rubric criteria (line following, obstacle handling, course completion) was met.
- Remind students this is the last session — collect any borrowed XRP hardware/kits at the end of class if applicable.
